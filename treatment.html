Short film treatment / 90 seconds

The Reaper Queue

An old woman in the underlevels keeps a key that says she trusts you. The last thing she does with it is sign for a stranger.

Runtime 1:30 hard cap Shots 19 Cast 3 Spoken lines 5 Locations 3

Structure

The whole film at a glance

Six movements. The emotional turn sits at 0:47, where the lights start going out, and the release lands at 1:10 when the key gets handed away instead of hidden. Everything before 0:47 exists to make that handoff cost something.

Cast

Three characters, one of them has no body

NULLA

60s / repairs dead formats

She built part of the network before it was worth owning. Now she runs a scrap kiosk in the underlevels, pulling voices off obsolete media for people who cannot afford to lose them. She is the last holder of a signing key that a few thousand people still treat as good.

Look
Close-cropped grey hair. One clouded eye from an implant that failed and was never replaced. A magnifier rig pushed up on her forehead. Solder burns across both hands. A quilted work coat with dozens of small pockets, each holding a different dead cartridge.
Lighting rule
Always lit by something in the room she could reach out and touch. Bench lamp, screen glow, the sodium tube overhead. Never a light with no source.

WREN

19 / courier

Raised entirely inside the data mall. Has never had a conversation nobody was allowed to read. She came down here to pick up a package and gets handed a responsibility instead. She does not understand what she is carrying until the last shot.

Look
Shaved sides, a braided data cable worn like hair. A cheap high visibility orange rain shell, the only saturated color in the film, so the audience can find her in any crowd. A corporate courier badge on a retractable line that she keeps flipping face down without noticing she does it.
Lighting rule
Underlit by her own handset in the first half. Lit by other people's screens in the last.

TALLY

Age unknown / has no body

A self replicating process that started as forty instructions in an abandoned research allocation and got complicated. It is near the top of a queue that deletes whatever has been running longest. It is not asking to be saved. It is asking to be carried.

Look
Never a body, never a hologram. A face assembled from a grid of small video thumbnails. Early in the film the tiles are stolen surveillance frames, cold and blue and slightly wrong, faces that never agreed. In the final shot the same face is built from the people who chose to carry it, warm and mismatched and clearly volunteered.
The whole arc
Same face structure throughout. Only the source pixels change. That single swap carries the ending with no dialogue required.

Look

Palette lock and lighting rules

Three colors carry the film and nothing else is allowed to be saturated. Sodium amber is the world people live in, dead cyan is the failing infrastructure, and the high visibility orange belongs to Wren alone so the eye can track her without help.

Sodium amber#E8A33D Every practical light in the underlevel. Warmth means someone is still living there.
Dead cyan#4FA3A8 One failing tube, screen backlight, the stolen surveillance tiles. Cold means machine looking at you.
Courier orange#FF5C1A Wren's shell only. Appears at 0:27 and vanishes into the crowd at 1:16.
Audit white#F3EDE1 Used exactly twice, at 0:47 and 0:56. Never appears anywhere else in the film.
  • Practical light only on faces. Every source is visible in frame or justified by something just outside it. This is what keeps generated skin from going plastic.
  • No camera shake anywhere. Impact comes from cut timing, from the light going out, and from holding the empty kiosk too long.
  • Main footage framing stays locked. Any push in or rise is slow and single direction. No alternating push and pull.
  • The Audit never gets a face, a body, a logo, or a voice. It is a wave of absence moving at walking pace.
  • Grain and dust in every underlevel shot. Clean frames only in the transit hall, which is the point of the transit hall.

Shots that need lipsync

Only five: 2, 6, 9, 10 and 19. Route those through S2V and leave the rest as image to video, which keeps the expensive path down to about 23 seconds of the runtime.

S2V runs the mouth ahead by the length of the audio's leading silence, so pad the head of each generated clip by that amount rather than retiming the audio. Shot 19 is the one that will show it worst, since the line lands over a cut to black.

Cut and QA order

Build the 0:47 to 1:05 stretch first. If the Audit does not land, nothing before it matters and the rest is wasted generation.

Before any handoff, contact sheet the first twelve seconds for dead black frames, then check that shot 17's overhead actually reads as spreading rather than as a static crowd. That one is the most likely to come back looking correct and feeling flat.
